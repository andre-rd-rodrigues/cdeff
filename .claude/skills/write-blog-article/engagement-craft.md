# Engagement craft

The reader should finish the article trusting that CDEFF knows what it is talking about and genuinely cares about how its young athletes grow. A parent should think *"this is the kind of club I want my child in."* An athlete should think *"these people can actually make me better, and I belong here."*

The pull comes from competence and care, never from a sales pitch. Unlike a commercial site, the blog page renders **no CTA and no booking button** after the body. So the article carries the whole relationship on its own, and it does that by being useful and human, not by asking for anything.

If the reader can point at the sentence that is selling the club, the article failed.

## What the page does and does not do

- The detail page shows the cover as a hero with the title, then the body, and then **nothing** — no related posts, no CTA, no contact block.
- So there is no template CTA to lean on and none to avoid colliding with. The body simply must not turn into an advert, because there is nothing to soften it.

## The mechanisms

Use two or three of these per article, spread out. Never stack them, never use all of them.

### 1. Competence shown, never claimed

Do not write *"no CDEFF somos especialistas em formação de atletas"*. Show it: give the practical detail a good coach would give, in the right order, and name the thing beginners get wrong.

> Muitos jovens saltam o aquecimento para começar a jogar mais depressa. É exatamente aí que aparecem as lesões que os deixam semanas de fora.

Precision reads as competence. Adjectives about the club read as marketing.

### 2. The formative frame

Once per article, connect the practical topic back to why it matters for a young person growing up, in the club's own terms: habits for life, discipline, wellbeing, belonging. This is the club's real differentiator, so state it as a fact about the sport, not as a boast about CDEFF.

> Aprender a descansar bem não serve só para render mais no próximo jogo. É um hábito que fica para a vida toda.

### 3. The club as the place this happens

At most once, and only where it is true, place CDEFF in the room as the environment where the athlete is supported — without a pitch.

> No dia a dia de um clube de formação, é o treinador que ajuda cada atleta a ajustar isto à sua idade e ao seu escalão.

Keep it generic about people: **no invented player stories, no results, no named athletes, no quotes.** A pattern a coach sees is fine; a specific child is not.

### 4. The belonging note

Once, near the end and before the conclusion, one plain sentence that says the young athlete is part of something and does not train alone. No poster slogans.

> No fundo, ninguém cresce sozinho no desporto: uma equipa e um clube fazem o caminho contigo.

Not: *"Junta-te já à família CDEFF!"*, *"Estamos à tua espera!"*, or anything that would fit on a banner.

### 5. The honest handoff

Where the topic genuinely depends on the individual athlete (age, escalão, medical history, body), say so plainly and point to the person who tailors it — the coach, or a professional like a sports nutritionist or doctor. The existing nutrition post does exactly this: *"Consultar um nutricionista desportivo pode ser uma ótima maneira de obter um plano personalizado."*

This is honest, useful, and it quietly shows the club sends people to the right expert instead of pretending to be all of them.

## Links

- One or two inline links to a **credible external reference** for a technical term (health authority, sports-science body, MSD Manuals, Wikipedia for a definition), as existing posts do.
- At most one link to a relevant **club page** where it genuinely helps the reader (for example the basketball or futsal page for someone who wants to try the sport). Never more, and never phrased as a service listing.
- Descriptive anchor text always. Never "clica aqui", "sabe mais", "este artigo".

## Dosage in a 400–900 word article

- 2–3 mechanisms, spread through the piece, never bunched at the end.
- 1–2 external reference links, at most 1 club-page link.
- 1 belonging note.
- 0 hard calls to action in the body.
- Everything else is pure, useful information. The first two thirds should give away genuinely useful material with no self-reference at all. Earn the trust first.

## Banned

- *Junta-te à família CDEFF*, *inscreve-te já*, *faz-te sócio hoje*, *não percas esta oportunidade* anywhere in the body.
- *Somos o melhor clube*, *especialistas de referência*, *anos de experiência comprovada* as boasts.
- *Estamos à tua espera* or any banner-ready closing.
- Any invented player story, result, trophy, statistic or testimonial.
- Fear or pressure framing, or invented urgency.
- More than one club-page link, or the same page linked twice.
- A conclusion that becomes a recruitment pitch instead of a genuine wrap-up of the topic.
